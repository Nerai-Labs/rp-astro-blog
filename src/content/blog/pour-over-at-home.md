Pour-over is the simplest way to taste what a roaster meant. This guide walks through the gear, the method and how to fix a cup that tastes off, in ten minutes a day.

## Why pour-over

Pour-over puts every variable in your hands: water, grind and time. Roasters like [Blue Bottle Coffee](https://bluebottlecoffee.com/) publish their recipes, and [Counter Culture](https://counterculturecoffee.com/) explains why fresh beans matter.

![A pour-over cone on a scale](../../assets/blog-placeholder-3.jpg)

### Equipment

- A cone, like the [Hario V60](https://www.hario-usa.com/)
- A gooseneck kettle
- A scale that reads to 0.1 g

Grind and bloom
---------------

Grind medium-fine, close to table salt. Pour twice the coffee's weight in water and wait 30 seconds while the gas escapes.

### Equipment

A burr grinder matters more than the cone. The [Baratza Encore](https://baratza.com/) is a common first one.

## Taste and adjust

Sour means under-extracted: grind finer. Bitter means over-extracted: grind coarser.

[Read the full brew guide](https://bluebottlecoffee.com/us/eng/brew-guides)

## Water

Use filtered water just off the boil, around 94 C. The [Specialty Coffee Association](https://sca.coffee/) publishes a water standard.

## Storing beans

Keep beans in an airtight bag away from light, and brew within a month of the roast date.

More recipes in [the blog](https://nerai-labs.github.io/rp-astro-blog/blog/).

## Questions

### How fine should I grind?

About like table salt, then adjust by taste.

### How long should it take?

Three to four minutes from the first pour.

Pour-over is the simplest way to taste what a roaster meant. This guide walks through the gear, the method and how to fix a cup that tastes off.

## Why pour-over

Pour-over puts every variable in your hands: water, grind and time. Change one of them and you taste the difference in the next cup, which makes it the fastest way to learn what you like.

Roasters like [Blue Bottle Coffee](https://bluebottlecoffee.com/) publish their recipes, and [Counter Culture](https://counterculturecoffee.com/) explains why fresh beans matter. Start with their numbers and adjust from there.

![A pour-over cone on a scale](../../assets/blog-placeholder-3.jpg)

### Equipment

- A cone, like the [Hario V60](https://www.hario-usa.com/)
- A gooseneck kettle
- A scale that reads to 0.1 g

Grind and bloom
---------------

Grind medium-fine, close to table salt. Too fine and the water stalls in the cone; too coarse and it runs through before it can pull out any sweetness.

Pour twice the coffee's weight in water and wait 30 seconds while the gas escapes. Fresh coffee bubbles and swells; coffee that lies flat is past its best.

### Grinder

A burr grinder matters more than the cone. The [Baratza Encore](https://baratza.com/) is a common first one, and it holds its setting from one morning to the next.

## Taste and adjust

Sour means under-extracted: grind finer. Bitter means over-extracted: grind coarser.

[Read the full brew guide](https://bluebottlecoffee.com/us/eng/brew-guides)

## Water

Coffee is almost all water, so the water you brew with shows up in the cup. Use filtered water just off the boil, around 94 C, and keep the kettle on the scale so every pour is measured.

Very hard water flattens the flavor and very soft water makes it sour. The [Specialty Coffee Association water standard](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards) lists the ranges roasters aim for.

## Storing beans

Keep beans in an airtight bag away from light, and brew within a month of the roast date.

More recipes and gear notes in [the blog](https://nerai-labs.github.io/rp-astro-blog/blog/).

## Questions

### How fine should I grind?

About like table salt to start. If the cup tastes sour, grind a little finer; if it tastes bitter, a little coarser.

### How long should it take?

Three to four minutes from the first pour to the last drip for a single cup.

### Do I need a gooseneck kettle?

It helps you pour slowly and evenly, but a steady hand with any kettle works for a first week.
